## Decompose the Problem

Do not ask “what caused the BIF?” as one question. Separate:

1. source of dissolved/particulate iron;
2. transport and storage of iron in the basin;
3. oxidation and precipitation pathway;
4. depositional concentration and dilution;
5. early diagenetic and metamorphic transformation;
6. relation to ice dynamics, open water, and deglaciation.

Different evidence is required for each step.

## Observation Inventory

Record mineralogy, textures, lamination/banding, grain size, detrital content,
facies contacts, lateral continuity, Fe valence/speciation when preserved,
major/trace elements, REE+Y patterns, Fe/C/S isotopes, organic carbon, sulfur
phases, alteration minerals, metamorphic grade, veins, and weathering state.

Distinguish primary chemical sediment, syndepositional clastic input,
diagenetic replacement, hydrothermal vein material, and supergene enrichment.

## Iron-Source Hypotheses

| Candidate source/process | Expected evidence | Non-unique controls |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrothermal input | Appropriate REE+Y patterns, vent-proximal gradients, basin/tectonic context | Diagenesis, detrital mixing, seawater modification |
| Continental or glacial weathering/runoff | Provenance link, detrital/weathering tracers, meltwater geometry | Dilution, recycling, facies change |
| Basinal or shelf sediment remobilization | Porewater/diagenetic release, redox shuttle, spatial gradients | Later diagenesis can mimic remobilization |
| Upwelling/overturn of ferruginous deep water | Paleogeographic pathway, redox stratification, repeated mixing-front deposition | Local restriction, changing oxidation front |

More than one source may operate. Source flux does not specify the oxidation
mechanism.

## Oxidation and Precipitation Hypotheses

Consider molecular oxygen supplied by photosynthesis or exchange, anoxygenic
photoferrotrophy, photochemical Fe(II) oxidation, mixing with another oxidant,
and local chemical conditions that trigger precipitation. Evaluate whether
light, oxidant supply, microbial metabolism, water depth, and reaction kinetics
are plausible at the depositional site.

Iron mineralogy in metamorphosed rocks may not preserve the original
precipitate. Do not identify a unique oxidant from bulk Fe enrichment alone.

## Glacial Linkage Tests

Test separately whether glaciation:

- enhanced iron supply through erosion, weathering, or meltwater;
- isolated a ferruginous reservoir beneath or beyond ice;
- changed circulation and allowed accumulation;
- created mixing/oxidation fronts during ice retreat or seasonal opening;
- changed sedimentation/dilution and preservation;
- merely provided the stratigraphic context while another process controlled
  iron deposition.

Require event order and overlap within age uncertainty. A CIF adjacent to a
diamictite is association, not proof of a particular deglacial mechanism.

## Proxy and Preservation Gates

- Petrographically separate primary, diagenetic, metamorphic, and vein phases.
- Quantify detrital contribution before using bulk trace-element ratios.
- Test weathered surfaces against fresh interiors.
- Treat Ce anomalies and Eu anomalies only after checking analytical method,
  detrital contamination, mineral hosts, and normalization.
- Interpret Fe isotopes with source, redox transformation, precipitation,
  transport, and diagenesis all considered.
- Interpret S and C records in their mineral/organic host context.
- Compare BIF samples with enclosing lithologies and facies-equivalent controls.

## Evidence Integration

Build two linked matrices:

1. **Iron budget:** sources -> transport/storage -> removal -> burial.
2. **Oxidation front:** oxidant source -> contact geometry -> reaction -> mineral
   record -> preservation.

Place each measurement in only the edge it directly tests. Use sedimentology to
constrain geometry and hydrodynamics; use geochemistry to constrain sources and
reactions; use chronology to test glacial coupling.

## Maximum Defensible Claims

- Fe-rich chemical sediment: substantial Fe supply and removal occurred locally.
- Primary redox/mineral evidence plus facies: a bounded oxidation/precipitation
  environment may be inferred.
- Multi-section, independently dated convergence: regional glacial coupling may
  be favored.
- CIF occurrence alone does not demonstrate global ocean anoxia, global
  oxygenation, persistent open water, or a unique iron source/oxidant.

## Common Failures

- Treating all Cryogenian iron formations as one genetic class.
- Conflating iron source with oxidation mechanism.
- Using metamorphic mineral assemblages as direct seawater precipitates.
- Interpreting bulk-rock signals without carrier phases or detrital controls.
- Turning stratigraphic adjacency into causal or globally synchronous linkage.
