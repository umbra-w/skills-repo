# Glacial Sedimentology and Open Water

Use this module to distinguish glacial influence, depositional process, ice
proximity, and open-water state. These are related but separate claims.

## Claim Ladder

Evaluate in order:

1. Was sediment or the basin influenced by ice?
2. Was deposition ice-contact, ice-proximal, ice-distal, or reworked?
3. Was liquid water present locally?
4. Was the water surface ice-free, seasonally open, intermittently open, or
   persistently open?
5. What spatial extent and duration are supported?
6. Does the record constrain a basin, regional ice margin, or global climate?

Evidence supporting rung 1 does not automatically support rungs 4-6.

## Glacial-Influence Evidence

| Observation | Direct sensitivity | Checks and alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Striated/faceted clasts with appropriate provenance and context | Ice transport or subglacial modification | Tectonic slickensides, mass flow, reworking |
| Outsized clast penetrating/deforming underlying laminae | Vertical emplacement such as ice rafting | Plant rafting, ejecta, debris flow, later intrusion; inspect geometry |
| Glacial abrasion surfaces, grooves, or striae | Ice-bed contact | Structural lineation and erosion; verify cross-cutting relations |
| Diamictite | Poor sorting and mixed grain sizes | Debris flow, mass transport, tectonic mixing, weathering; not diagnostic alone |
| Repeated ice-advance/retreat facies architecture | Dynamic ice-margin behavior | Requires mapped contacts, paleoflow, and sequence context |
| Ice-rafted debris distribution | Floating ice or other rafted transport | Quantify clast size, frequency, provenance, matrix, and reworking |

Use facies associations and architecture, not isolated textbook structures.

## Open-Water Evidence Classes

### Hydrodynamic evidence

Wave ripples, combined-flow structures, hummocky or swaley cross-stratification,
traction-current beds, tidal bundles, and shoreface/deltaic architecture can
support open or weakly ice-covered water when their process interpretation and
facies context are secure. Some currents operate beneath ice shelves or through
leads; a current structure alone does not prove persistent ice-free surface.

### Ice-water interaction evidence

Co-occurrence of ice-rafted debris with wave/current structures may support an
ice margin, seasonal opening, leads, or polynya-like water. Establish whether
features are syndepositional and share the same bed/interval rather than being
reworked from different conditions.

### Photochemical or biological evidence

Signals requiring light, photosynthesis, or air-water exchange may support an
illuminated surface environment but are not automatically diagnostic of an
ice-free surface. Light penetration through thin ice, cracks, seasonal melt,
and transported material remain alternatives. Pair them with sedimentology.

### Paleogeographic evidence

Lateral facies belts, basin geometry, provenance, and ice-flow direction can
test whether open water was local to an ice margin or extensive. A single
vertical section rarely fixes areal extent.

## Open-Water Classification

| Class | Minimum evidence | Maximum claim |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid-water deposition | Depositional processes requiring liquid water | Liquid water at the site; surface state unresolved |
| Intermittent/seasonal opening | Repeated open-water facies interbedded with glacial/ice-influenced facies | Local opening during sampled intervals |
| Persistent local open water | Sustained facies architecture plus independent exchange/light evidence and no incompatible ice cover | Persistent at the locality over the preserved interval |
| Basin-scale open water | Correlated evidence across paleogeographic transects and time-equivalent facies | Open-water domain within the basin |
| Regional/global state | Multiple basins, independent ages, climate-compatible geometry | Broader state with explicit model dependence |

Use “open water” with an adjective: local, intermittent, seasonal, persistent,
or basin-scale. If duration is unresolved, say so.

## Preservation and Context Gates

- Confirm primary bedding and exclude tectonic fabrics or weathering artifacts.
- Document bed contacts, younging direction, soft-sediment deformation, and
  cross-cutting relations.
- Test mass-transport and turbidite alternatives.
- Check whether clasts or structures are reworked.
- Relate observations to a measured log and facies association.
- Align sedimentary and geochemical samples at compatible resolution.

## Maximum Defensible Claims

- A diamictite alone: poorly sorted deposit; glacial origin remains a hypothesis.
- A validated dropstone: rafted emplacement; rafting agent and surface duration
  need additional evidence.
- Wave/current facies plus ice-rafted debris: local open or weakly ice-covered
  water compatible with an ice margin; not global deglaciation.
- Repeated, correlated facies across a transect: spatially bounded open-water
  reconstruction; still not a global Snowball Earth verdict.

## Common Failures

- Treating every diamictite as tillite.
- Equating liquid water beneath/among ice with an ice-free ocean.
- Using one structure to infer persistence or areal extent.
- Ignoring redeposition, mass flow, or sub-ice-shelf circulation.
- Calling an interval synchronous without independent age control.

## Integration Needs

Integrate sedimentology with paleogeography, provenance, age control, Hg or
other photochemical indicators, redox depth structure, and replication across
facies. State which evidence constrains physical water state and which only
records chemistry or preservation.
