## Age Evidence Is Not Interchangeable

Record the actual dating relation rather than only an age number:

| Evidence | What it directly constrains | Key limitations to record |
|---|---|---|
| U-Pb age from demonstrably primary ash/tuff | Depositional horizon close to crystallization age | Reworking, inheritance, Pb loss, horizon placement, analytical uncertainty |
| Intrusive rock age | Intrusion/emplacement | Only a maximum or minimum sedimentary bound depending on field relation |
| Re-Os or other depositional authigenic age | Formation of the analyzed authigenic system | Initial composition, detrital component, closure and alteration |
| Detrital-zircon youngest population | Maximum depositional age | Sampling, reworking, population criteria; not depositional age by default |
| Magnetostratigraphy or astrochronology | Correlation/relative duration under a valid framework | Remagnetization, tuning assumptions, cycle preservation |
| Chemostratigraphic pattern | Candidate correlation | Non-uniqueness, facies/redox effects, diachroneity, alteration |
| Lithostratigraphic similarity | Relative field correlation hypothesis | Facies migration and recurrence; never proves synchronicity alone |

Do not rank a method without considering its field relationship, archive,
uncertainty, and preservation.

## Age Model Audit

For every numeric age, capture:

1. dated material and mineral;
2. method and reference standard;
3. analytical uncertainty and confidence convention;
4. field/stratigraphic relation to the target bed;
5. evidence for reworking, inheritance, open-system behavior, or alteration;
6. whether it is direct, maximum, minimum, interpolated, or correlated;
7. source pointer and whether the primary study was inspected.

Build intervals, not false point ages. Preserve asymmetric or method-specific
uncertainties where reported.

## Correlation Ladder

Advance only when the lower rung is supported:

1. **Within bed/sample:** analytical and petrographic coherence.
2. **Within section:** measured stratigraphic order, thickness, contacts, hiatuses.
3. **Among local sections:** marker beds, facies architecture, and replicated trends.
4. **Across basin:** independent age anchors plus sequence/facies framework.
5. **Across regions/globally:** multiple radiometric anchors and a tested
   correlation model robust to diachroneity.

Chemistry can assist correlation but must not both define the correlation and
serve as independent evidence for the event inferred from that correlation.

## Temporal Alignment Test

Before correlating two records, report:

```text
Record A resolution and uncertainty:
Record B resolution and uncertainty:
Possible overlap interval:
Hiatus/condensation/reworking risk:
Correlation basis independent of target proxy:
Verdict: aligned / possibly aligned / unresolved / not aligned
```

Do not claim cause precedes effect when their uncertainty intervals cannot
resolve the order. “Same formation” or “postglacial” is usually too coarse for a
short-lived causal sequence.

## Duration and Rate

Calculate a rate only when both interval thickness/quantity and elapsed time are
independently constrained. Propagate age and measurement uncertainty. Check for
condensation, hiatus, erosion, variable sedimentation, compaction, and sampling
resolution. If these dominate, report an order-of-magnitude range or withhold
the rate.

## Diachroneity Tests

Consider whether a boundary or facies migrated through space. Evidence for
diachroneity includes inconsistent radiometric brackets, systematic facies-
dependent position, transgressive/regressive migration, or basin-specific
hiatuses. A glacial retreat surface, redox boundary, or first appearance may be
time-transgressive even when visually distinctive.

## Allowed Claim Levels

| Chronology state | Maximum claim |
|---|---|
| Direct bracketing ages with overlap and replicated correlation | Bounded event order or regional synchronicity |
| One direct age plus coherent local stratigraphy | Local timing with explicit extrapolation limits |
| Chemo/lithostratigraphic correlation only | Candidate correlation, not precise synchronicity |
| Detrital maximum age only | Deposition occurred after the stated bound |
| No usable age control | Facies- or section-relative sequence only |

## Common Failures

- Treating a maximum depositional age as the depositional age.
- Ignoring the stratigraphic distance between dated and interpreted horizons.
- Correlating on the same anomaly later used as evidence of global forcing.
- Reporting more temporal precision than sampling spacing permits.
- Inferring global synchronicity from formation names or lithologic resemblance.

## Completion Check

Every event-order, duration, rate, and cross-section statement must identify its
age evidence, uncertainty, correlation rung, diachroneity risk, and maximum
defensible temporal claim.
