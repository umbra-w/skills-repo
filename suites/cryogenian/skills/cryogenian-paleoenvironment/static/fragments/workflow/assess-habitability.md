# Assess Habitability

## Inputs

Accept environmental observations or reconstructions plus any fossils,
biomarkers, organic matter, or ecological claims.

## Actions

1. Define the organism or metabolism whose habitat requirements are being
   assessed; avoid a universal habitability label.
2. Evaluate liquid/open water, redox structure, electron donors/acceptors,
   nutrients, salinity, temperature, light, stability, toxicants, substrate,
   and preservation using
   [deglaciation and habitability](../../../references/deglaciation-and-habitability.md).
3. Separate environmental potential from biological presence, activity,
   abundance, ecological expansion, and preservation.
4. Audit biosignature syngeneity, specificity, contamination, reworking,
   maturity, and stratigraphic alignment.
5. Compare a biological-occupation hypothesis with abiotic, prokaryote-dominated,
   transported, contaminated, and preservation-bias alternatives.
6. State which claim level is supported and design the next decisive test.

## Required Checks

- Favorable redox or nutrient conditions do not prove occupation.
- Fossil or biomarker presence does not by itself prove local habitability or
  causal response to deglaciation.
- Temporal coincidence is tested within age uncertainty.

## Output

Return a habitability-dimension table, occupation evidence audit, alternatives,
bounded conclusion, and hypothesis test.

## Stop Condition

When evidence supports only environmental potential, stop at “compatible with”
or “more/less favorable for” and do not infer organisms were present.
