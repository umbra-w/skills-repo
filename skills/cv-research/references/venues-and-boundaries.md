# Venues and Boundaries

## Venue-aware evaluation

Use venue context to shape evidence and presentation, but verify current official policies before giving deadline, page-limit, anonymity, supplementary, code, dataset, or generative-AI guidance.

- CVPR, ICCV, ECCV: emphasize visual task relevance, technical contribution, strong empirical comparison, qualitative diagnostics, and reproducibility.
- NeurIPS, ICLR: often demand a clear learning contribution, broader methodological insight, or rigorous analysis beyond one application.
- AAAI and adjacent venues: assess fit using the current call and track rather than stereotypes.
- Journals: distinguish initial submission from revision cycles and verify journal-specific scope and formatting.

Do not claim that a result “meets CVPR level.” Instead identify which reviewer expectations are satisfied, unsupported, or risky.

## Task boundaries

- Route literature discovery to an academic-search skill when current prior art must be found; return here afterward to synthesize the gap and experiment plan.
- Route implementation-only work to coding or experiment-management tools, while retaining this skill for hypotheses, controls, and interpretation.
- Route figure rendering to a scientific-figure skill after defining the visual claim and data contract here.
- Route PDF, slides, spreadsheets, or document production to the corresponding artifact skill.
- Never let artifact polish substitute for missing scientific evidence.

## Submission readiness

Check:

- claim-evidence alignment;
- closest-prior-work positioning;
- fair and sufficient baselines;
- controlled ablations and alternative explanations;
- data provenance and leakage audit;
- uncertainty, effect sizes, and subgroup/failure analysis;
- compute and efficiency disclosure;
- reproducibility details and artifact consistency;
- anonymity and current venue rules;
- limitations, ethics, licenses, consent, and privacy as applicable;
- consistency across abstract, tables, figures, supplement, code, and rebuttal.
