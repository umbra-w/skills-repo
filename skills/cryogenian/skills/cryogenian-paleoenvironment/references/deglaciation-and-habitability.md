# Deglaciation and Habitability

Use this module to separate deglacial environmental change, potential
habitability, biological presence, ecological response, and preservation.

## Deglaciation Is a Set of State Changes

Track ice extent/proximity, open-water state, temperature, sea level,
freshwater input, salinity, circulation, stratification, gas exchange, redox,
weathering/runoff, nutrients, productivity, and sedimentation separately. Their
boundaries may be diachronous and their trajectories non-monotonic.

Do not treat a stratigraphic label such as “postglacial” as a precise time slice.

## Organism-Specific Habitability

Habitability depends on the organism or metabolism. Evaluate:

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Liquid/open water | Was accessible liquid water present, and was light/gas exchange required? |
| Energy and redox | Which electron donors/acceptors and redox interfaces existed? |
| Nutrients | Were bioavailable N, P, Fe, or trace nutrients supplied and retained? |
| Physical conditions | Were salinity, temperature, water depth, energy, and substrate compatible? |
| Temporal stability | Did suitable conditions persist at biologically relevant duration? |
| Toxicity/stress | Did sulfide, metals, acidity, oxidants, or osmotic stress limit habitat? |
| Connectivity | Could organisms disperse into or between suitable habitats? |
| Preservation | Could biosignatures survive deposition, diagenesis, metamorphism, and weathering? |

Favorable change in one dimension does not establish overall habitability.

## Claim Ladder

1. **Environmental state:** observations constrain water, redox, nutrients, etc.
2. **Potential habitability:** conditions are compatible with a specified
   organism/metabolism.
3. **Biological presence:** syngenetic evidence indicates organisms were present.
4. **Activity/productivity:** evidence indicates metabolism or biomass production.
5. **Ecological expansion:** abundance/diversity/distribution increased.
6. **Causal response:** deglacial change drove that expansion.

Each rung requires new evidence. Do not jump from rung 1 or 2 to rung 3-6.

## Biosignature Gates

### Microfossils

Check morphology, wall structure, division or organization, taxonomic
specificity, orientation, occurrence within primary laminae, reworking,
contamination, metamorphic state, and stratigraphic replication. Morphology may
be ambiguous; preserve alternative biological and abiotic origins.

### Biomarkers

Check sample interiors, blanks, drilling/preparation contamination, laboratory
materials, thermal maturity, indigenous spatial distribution, syngeneity,
transport, and taxonomic specificity. A molecular structure may have multiple
sources or younger contamination pathways.

### Bulk organic and isotope signals

These can constrain carbon/nutrient cycling or productivity under explicit
models but rarely identify eukaryotic occupation alone. Evaluate preservation,
source mixing, methane cycling, and facies.

## Environmental Versus Preservation Effects

An apparent postglacial biological increase may reflect true ecological change,
improved preservation, increased sediment exposure, facies migration, sampling
bias, transported material, or chronological resolution. Test abundance and
preservation indicators across comparable facies and multiple sections.

## Causal Test

For “deglaciation improved habitability and enabled X,” require:

1. a dated environmental change;
2. a specified physiological/ecological link to X;
3. biological evidence aligned within uncertainty;
4. spatial replication where the environmental gradient predicts response;
5. preservation and sampling controls;
6. alternatives such as dispersal, facies change, and preservational bias;
7. a prediction that differs under the causal and non-causal models.

## Verdict Vocabulary

- `compatible with potential habitability for X`;
- `more/less favorable for X than the comparison interval`;
- `evidence supports local presence of X`;
- `evidence supports activity/productivity under stated assumptions`;
- `causal link unresolved` or `favored by discriminating evidence`.

Avoid “habitable ocean” without organism, place, interval, and dimensions.

## Maximum Defensible Claim

Geochemical and sedimentological changes can establish environmental potential
or relative favorability. Demonstrated occupation requires syngenetic,
specific, contamination-controlled biological evidence. Causal response to
deglaciation additionally requires resolved timing, mechanism, spatial pattern,
and alternatives.

## Common Failures

- Equating oxygenation or nutrient availability with eukaryotic presence.
- Treating an isolated biomarker as contamination-free and taxonomically unique.
- Ignoring anoxia as both a stressor and possible preservation aid.
- Assuming biological and geochemical samples are synchronous.
- Calling increased preservation an ecological radiation.
- Treating “early life” as one metabolism with one environmental optimum.
